2b. This website, and what it loads

The statement above concerns the processing of email. This website itself loads two third-party services, and it is better to say so than to let you find out:

  • Cal.com — the booking calendar, loaded in an isolated frame and only as you approach that section. A visitor who never scrolls there never contacts Cal.com. That frame may set its own cookies, over which we have no control.
  • Web3Forms — contact-form delivery. Contacted only on submit, never on page load.
  • Google Fonts — three typefaces. An enhancement, not a dependency: without them the page stays readable and correctly set.

We set no tracking cookie of our own and use no analytics tool. The only value this site writes to your browser is your language choice, kept locally, which never leaves your machine.

3. Why, and on what legal basis

  • Purpose: to qualify your commercial enquiry and prepare a personalised reply.
  • Legal basis: pre-contractual steps taken at your request, and our legitimate interest in handling enquiries efficiently (GDPR art. 6.1(b) and 6.1(f)).
  • No fully automated decision-making: a person reads and approves every proposal before anything is written to the CRM or any reply is drafted (GDPR art. 22). The system drafts and waits; it never acts alone.

5. How long we keep it

Your data is kept for 365 days by default from the date we receive it, then automatically purged from the CRM, from the system's technical memory and from the processing queues. Cached company profiles and the internal knowledge base contain no personal data and are not affected.

6. Your rights

  • Access — know what data we hold about you.
  • Rectification — correct inaccurate data.
  • Erasure — request deletion before the normal expiry.
  • Portability — receive your data in a structured format.
  • Objection — object to processing on legitimate grounds.

To exercise a right, write to . We answer within one month (GDPR art. 12.3); if a request is complex we tell you so inside that month rather than letting the deadline pass in silence.

Erasure is not a promise kept by hand: it is implemented. A per-person erasure command exists in the product (retention.py --oublier) and removes the CRM rows, the internal state holding the email body, the queues and the follow-up tracking, returning a count per location — which is what lets us answer you precisely instead of asserting that it is done.

The audit log is deliberately kept (legitimate interest, art. 17.3(e)): it records who approved what and when, and deleting a row would erase the trace along with the data. That is a stated decision, not an oversight.

You may also lodge a complaint with the competent supervisory authority: .

7. Security

  • Every write to the CRM is preceded by an explicit human approval.
  • Access to the internal tool is password-protected, with progressive lockout after repeated failures and a second factor for administrator accounts.
  • Every human approval is logged — who, what, when — in a hash-chained journal.
  • API keys are never stored in the code or shown on screen.

8. If there is a data breach

If a breach of your data creates a risk to your rights, we notify the competent authority within 72 hours (GDPR art. 33) and inform you directly where the risk is high (art. 34). We will tell you what happened, which data was involved and what we did about it — not merely that an incident occurred.

On a client deployment the client notifies, since they are the controller; acami assists and supplies without delay whatever the hash-chained journal can establish.

9. Children

This service is aimed at professionals. It is not intended for people under 16 and we do not knowingly collect their data. If you believe that has happened, write to us and we will erase it.

10. Data Processing Agreement

For a client deploying ACA, a Data Processing Agreement meeting GDPR art. 28 is available on request: it sets the scope, the duration, the sub-processors, the security measures and what happens to the data at the end of the engagement. Ask for it before signing rather than after — that is when it is useful.

5. What we do not warrant

ACA relies on language models, whose outputs are probabilistic. We do not warrant the accuracy of any classification, any extracted field or any drafted text. That is precisely why nothing reaches your CRM or a customer without a person having read and approved it: the human approval is not a formality, it is the product's correction mechanism.

Nor do we warrant the availability of the third-party services the product depends on (model providers, mail, CRM), or the continuation of their free tiers.

6. Liability

Our liability is limited to the amount actually paid for the engagement concerned. We are not liable for indirect damages, including lost revenue or lost customers. No limitation applies in cases of gross negligence or wilful misconduct, or where the law forbids it.

7. Confidentiality and ownership

The data and documents you entrust to us remain yours and are used only for the engagement. The ACA codebase and its generic evolutions remain our property; the configuration, content and knowledge base specific to your deployment are yours and are handed over on delivery.

Text produced by the system is yours. Proposals, replies, indicative quotes: you hold the rights to them, royalty-free and without time limit, and you may use them after the engagement ends as during it. We claim nothing over what the machine writes to your customers — that would be claiming your commercial correspondence.

We do not use your data to train a model. The product trains nothing: it calls models hosted by third parties, under those parties' terms, with your own API keys. The (draft, corrected version) pairs the system records stay with you, in your database, and exist only to show you what was changed.

8. Standing under the EU AI Act

ACA is a general-purpose AI system applied to assisted drafting, under continuous human supervision. It falls neither within the prohibited practices nor within Annex III's high-risk systems: it evaluates no natural person and decides no access to employment, credit, an essential service or a public benefit.

The obligation that actually applies is transparency (art. 50): a person receiving text produced with AI assistance must be able to know it. You are responsible for that in your use, since you are the one sending. The product helps you meet it rather than excusing you from it — nothing goes out without a person having read it, and the journal records who approved what.

04 — Acceptable Use

Acceptable Use.

Last updated: 2026-08-07

ACA reads inbound email and drafts replies. That capability can be used to work well or to do harm, and this section states which of the two the engagement covers.

What ACA is for

  • Triaging, qualifying and preparing replies to email that people have deliberately sent to you.
  • Drafting proposals that someone on your team reads and takes responsibility for before sending.

What is prohibited

  • Bulk unsolicited messaging, or processing addresses obtained without a lawful basis.
  • Removing, bypassing or automating the human approval step — including approving without reading. It is the product's central guarantee and the basis of several statements in the privacy policy.
  • Passing off generated text as a person's own analysis, or presenting a generated reply as professional advice that has not been checked.
  • Processing special categories of data (health, opinions, orientation) without the specific safeguards the regulation requires.
  • Attempting to extract, reverse-engineer or resell the system, or using it to attack a third party.
  • Circumventing model providers' usage limits, or running the system at a volume that amounts to reselling access to those models.
  • Using the system on subjects where a drafting error affects a person's health, safety or rights — medical, legal or financial advice delivered as-is to a third party.

Security testing

Testing the security of your own deployment is legitimate and we encourage it: it runs on your infrastructure. Tell us before an intrusive test if a care subscription is running, so an alert does not fire into the void. Testing acami's infrastructure, or another client's, without written authorisation is not covered by that permission.

What happens if this is breached

An acceptable-use policy that states no consequence is not a rule, it is a wish. In order:

  1. We write to you, describing precisely what we observed and what needs to change. Most breaches are misunderstandings and stop here.
  2. We suspend the care subscription if it continues. The system itself stays with you and keeps running: we do not switch off a tool that belongs to you — we stop contributing to it.
  3. We end the relationship for a serious or repeated breach — in particular bypassing human approval, which is the guarantee everything else on these pages rests on.
  4. We report to the competent authorities where the law requires it. We will not do so lightly, and we will tell you, unless the law forbids us to.
